1. A large-scale study of Google’s AI Overviews finds citation grounding has nothing to do with whether the answer is right.

(single-source — arXiv preprint, submitted Oct 29 2026)

Across 3,611 benchmark health questions, Overviews appeared 96.4% of the time, cited pages scoring lower than top-5 results on four of six credibility criteria, and leaned toward commercial publishers over academic sources — and “the accuracy of AIOs was unrelated to the grounding of their statements.”

On a physician rubric, Overviews scored just 38.2% of available points, and 22.2% of anticipated-harmful-claim answers contained it. For any interface using citations to build trust: showing a source proves nothing about correctness.

2. A Toyota Research Institute/MIT shared-driving study finds the design element that reads as trustworthy is haptic steering coupling you can feel and override — most measured safety gains don’t clear significance.

(single-source — arXiv preprint, submitted Oct 26 2026)

Drivers rated “AI knew my intent to pass” M=5.33 (p<.001) and “AI’s action was safe” M=4.25 (p=.002), but “AI helped me perform the maneuver” was flat (p=.666). Steering is physically coupled — the driver feels the torque and can override at any moment — while throttle and braking are not; that’s the pattern that survives.

Caveat: the paper’s own urban-driving evaluation separately reports the AI increasing collisions, attributed to “risk compensation and residual policy errors.”

5. A single-author study measures how long a computer-use agent’s screenshot-then-click plan stays valid, and most agents wait the wrong amount of time.

(single-source — arXiv preprint, submitted Oct 29 2026)

Only 0.4% of clicks planned on a fully loaded page go stale within 30 seconds, against 17% planned in the first second after a page opens — and eight of ten widely used open-source agents’ wait rules leave over 1% of clicks stale regardless.

Fix: pixel comparison verifies a click target as accurately as a frontier vision-language model, at one-eighth the latency. Single-author, two days old — a lead, not settled, but the number is usable now.
