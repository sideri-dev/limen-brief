1. EvoMaestro gives experts slider-level steering handles into an LLM-driven program-evolution search, and a 12-person study shows it works.

(single-source, accepted ACM UIST 2026)

A formative study with 8 domain experts produced six design requirements; the resulting tool lets a person guide the search with natural language, combine promising directions, and prune unproductive ones — “semantic oversight” between opaque autorun and full manual control. A 12-participant within-subjects study found it improves understanding of the evolution process and reduces cognitive workload while still supporting real steering, with a 7-day single-expert field demo reported alongside, though not as a formal study.

Generalizes past this tool: any agentic or generative search product needs a mid-process control surface below “see the final result.”

2. Feeling in control of an AI’s first move doesn’t mean you feel responsible for what it did — that’s a separate, reflective judgment.

(single-source)

Two experiments found people experience immediate “I caused that” agency over the initial action a delegated system takes, but not over its final outcome — yet they do judge themselves to have caused the outcome when their original command was specific and the result mattered. The authors are blunt: designers “cannot rely on pre-reflective intuition” to produce ownership over delegated outcomes.

For an approval or confirmation step in an agentic product, that means echoing the user’s own prior instruction back at them when showing a result — not just displaying what happened — is what produces reflective ownership.

3. Locking an AI writing tool’s generation behind a measured “meaningful engagement” threshold shifts effort toward writing and evaluating, without making the task take longer.

(single-source)

A controlled 398-person experiment, four conditions including a timing-matched control isolating the engagement requirement from simple delay, found this redistributed effort: more time writing, less time evaluating, and the best accuracy-per-time evaluation efficiency of any condition, without lengthening the overall task.

A real alternative to the two defaults most generation UIs use — a plain unlocked button or a fixed-timer rate limit: measure engagement, not time.

4. A robot that deliberately speaks haltingly to English learners really does improve approachability, reduce anxiety, AND raise motivation — a researcher error nearly reported the opposite.

(single-source, non-peer-reviewed poster — corrected)

The original write-up of this HAI Symposium poster mapped its one non-significant result onto “motivation” — a genuinely counterintuitive finding, had it been true. Reading the full Japanese text off the page, not the results figure, shows the opposite: motivation is significant (p=0.026), with approachability (p=0.0035) and anxiety reduction (p=0.001) — all three benefits the abstract claims hold up. The actual non-significant result, continued-use intention (p=0.18), is something the abstract never claimed; it splits participants by whether they value the robot’s functional accuracy or emotional support, with no net benefit either way.

The lesson sharpens on correction: a hesitant “weak robot” persona is validated for a coaching agent on first contact, but before betting on it for sustained use, know which way your users split.

5. Notion keeps an AI agent’s authoritative context in page bodies, not database properties, after the properties got overloaded — and a Slack correction is a trigger to investigate, never permission to rewrite a number.

(single-source, vendor-primary)

Notion’s own engineering account describes metadata fields that got “harder to maintain” once asked to carry curated context alongside structured data, where “a query could run cleanly and still answer the wrong question.” Their fix separates the two: context lives in page bodies, read by a scoped Custom Agent, not a database automation.

The more exportable decision: an informal correction to an agent’s output is a signal to investigate the underlying data, never a license to silently patch the number it got wrong.
