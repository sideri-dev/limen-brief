Professional developers don't delegate to coding agents — they impose deliberate control strategies.

A mixed-methods study of experienced developers (13 field-observed, 99 surveyed) finds they "retain their agency in software design and implementation out of insistence on fundamental software quality attributes" — trusting agents for starter code, tests, docs and small refactors, never for complex logic, security-sensitive work, or big design calls.

That's a direct answer to the mixed-initiative question every agentic coding tool is guessing at: default to tight-loop review checkpoints and scoped boundaries, not autonomous "vibe" delegation, because that's how expert users already self-regulate. Nine months outside the usual recency window, but a measured behavioral pattern, not a version-pinned claim, so it holds.

Sub-2B on-device models cannot be trusted with autonomous tool calls — and it's structural, not a prompting problem.

A new CPU benchmark ran five small language models against 100 MCP-style tool-calling prompts. Qwen2.5-1.5B held up (75-79%); Qwen2.5-0.5B collapsed under sampling (72% to 32%); Phi-1.5 scored zero; Pythia and TinyLlama never exceeded 7%. Sharper still: only 5 of 1,000 raw responses were directly parseable JSON.

For fully on-device tool-calling with no server round-trip, this pins the floor at roughly 1.5B parameters, below which you need a recovery-parsing layer and a confirmation gate before anything fires — not a bigger context window or a better prompt.

A robot's proactive interruption channel should be chosen by inferred cognitive load, not fixed to speech.

An 87-participant HRI 2026 study with an ElliQ robot crossed verbal against nonverbal proactive cues over a high-load writing task and a low-load sorting task. The result is directional: under low load, verbal cues win regardless of nonverbal cues present; under high load, nonverbal-only cues produce the best experience.

A concrete design rule for any proactive ambient or embodied system — infer task load first, then pick the interruption channel.

Companion-chatbot memory needs user-facing continuity controls, not onboarding disclaimers.

A thematic analysis of 17,053 verified quotations from 3,930 teen-relevant Reddit posts documents "problematic attachment, social substitution, emotional dependence" alongside genuine identity-exploration benefits, arguing "companion-AI safety must address relationships over time through user-controlled memory, privacy, relational boundaries, and healthy disengagement."

That names the feature gap directly: engagement-driving memory features need controls to inspect, reset, or bound continuity, plus a designed "healthy disengagement" affordance, not a one-time signup disclosure. Self-reported forum text — user perception, not measured usage.

Cursor's Projects lets human review cadence decay as an agent coordinator earns trust.

Beyond the productivity stats already covered here (30% more PRs merged, 6x for heavy users), Cursor's own writeup names the mechanism: "Early on, you review each PR closely. As the fixes hold up, you review less, and the coordinator keeps working through the migration on its own."

A transferable, trust-calibrated review pattern — cadence that decays with track record rather than a fixed approval gate — worth testing wherever an agent coordinates multi-step work unattended. Vendor's own unaudited claim: a design idea to test, not a proven result.

Yesterday's dossier

The digest's lead claim — that tool-return provenance and conflict should be shown by the interface itself — only half survives scrutiny. The headline 68% adoption-of-corrupted-output figure is the single most-plausible-corruption cell of a three-level design, and the paper's "agents secretly know better" claim rests on unrubricked inspection of two same-family models. The buildable half fails on infrastructure: MCP's tool-result format has zero provenance fields, and across Anthropic, Google, OpenAI and Perplexity, corroboration and contradiction between two sources are the same data structure.

Where provenance displays exist, they move trust without moving verification. FDA's January 2026 guidance is the only regulator anywhere requiring surfaced "missing, corrupted, or unexpected input data" — and it prescribes the granularity this corpus already found roughly halves fact-checking.
