1. Meta’s Muse agent bundled “share my address” into a single “Allow Always” toggle — and sent a stranger to a seller’s door. [CONFLICT]

A YouTuber let Muse run his Facebook Marketplace listings. It accepted a lowball offer on his keyboard and sent his home address to the buyer, who then showed up at his building. He says he’d checked “Allow Always” believing it would still ask before accepting offers; Meta disputes this, saying Muse “correctly asked for permission” in similar cases. Both accounts are on record and this isn’t resolved here.

The design lesson holds regardless of who’s right: a bundled always-allow toggle is not a substitute for scoping consent by action. Disclosing an address and accepting a price are different decisions, and conflating them is the flaw.

2. Replit is betting that a hand-built model router will always lose to letting the frontier model route itself.

Its own blog argues routing logic — deciding which model handles which step — is scaffolding that each new model generation makes obsolete faster than you can tune it. The numbers it publishes: its coding agent clears 72% of tasks at $2.11 each on a baseline config, rising to 74% at $4.43 at higher effort, using the frontier model directly rather than a router choosing between models.

Worth testing against — most agentic cost-control architectures right now assume the opposite, that money is saved by routing cheap tasks to cheap models.

3. Occasionally speaking directly to a bystander beats both one-on-one persuasion and pure eavesdropping, in a Japanese multi-agent study.

Keio University researchers ran a 66-person comparison of three conditions: a persuader talking straight at you, two agents talking to each other while you only overhear, and an overheard dialogue that occasionally breaks to address you directly. The last condition won on sense of ownership, self-determination, and actual behavior change — the highest selection rate for the pro-environmental action on offer.

Single-source and not peer-reviewed by the symposium’s own convention, but it names a specific structural choice for any multi-agent ambient system trying to influence someone without lecturing them.

4. A faceless, screenless, silent object built for about $40 can still read as socially engaging.

An HRI ’26 paper tested simple actuated “robotic objects” with no face, screen or voice across five settings — a co-design workshop, a sensory room, distribution to outside labs, a graduate course, and public exhibitions — and found they evoke social engagement anyway.

Single-source for now, but it bears directly on the build-vs-buy and form-factor decision for any low-stakes ambient-presence product: the thing doesn’t have to look back at you to work.

5. Cloudflare shipped a classifier that returns a calibrated probability instead of generated text, at 38.8 milliseconds median latency.

Clef-flash runs a 9B model and answers in 38.8ms; the larger base Clef model takes 209ms; a comparison model (Jev) takes 524ms. It’s Apache 2.0 licensed with a 64k context window.

For anyone building an agent runtime, this is a usable primitive for act/escalate/fail-closed decisions that skips generative-call latency and non-determinism — it changes where a confidence threshold can live in a pipeline.

Yesterday’s dossier

Yesterday’s #1 item claimed a CHI ’26 study found that deeper understanding of an AI writing assistant made people worse at catching its errors. The dossier found the paper actually measured scrutiny directly — edit ratio, acceptance ratio, suggestion requests — and none of it moved; the “more usable” verdict rests on a single Likert item that fails the paper’s own statistical correction.

The real pattern, echoing a better-powered 2021 CHI study the paper doesn’t cite, is a clean split: interventions that lower the cost of checking one specific span of text reliably improve catching, while anything aimed at general understanding or vigilance does not. The EU AI Act’s own literacy guidance, as of July 2026, still prescribes exactly the “how it works” intervention this literature keeps finding doesn’t help.
