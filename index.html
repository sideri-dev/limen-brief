1. A new benchmark shows the field’s own turn-taking metrics reward the wrong behavior in voice AI.

TACT scores turn-taking against six latent-intent classes instead of binary silence/overlap rules, across 9,728 episodes and 73.2 hours from five dyadic corpora. The best of eleven systems tested reaches only 0.47 against a 0.86 human topline, and agrees with human judgment at Spearman 0.81 versus 0.46 for the binary metrics most products QA against — every system shows an over-eagerness pathology on reflective turns.

If a voice product leans on latency or takeover-rate as its turn-taking KPI, this is the documented case the metric optimizes the wrong thing; silence-tolerance needs testing per-intent, not minimized across the board. Single-source preprint, two-person team — a strong lead, not a settled result.

2. Agent-initiated interruption on a shared document raised output quality with zero cognitive-load cost.

DocuTeam’s agents monitor a live document and proactively start or redirect discussion as the work evolves, rather than waiting to be invoked. In a within-subjects study of 20 participants, outcomes were rated significantly more novel, relevant and specific than a baseline — with no increase in perceived cognitive load, the usual argument against unsolicited AI interruption.

Small n and one domain, so treat it as a lead on interruption timing, not a rule — but it’s evidence for letting an agent surface a prompt on document change rather than gating every intervention behind a request.

3. How domain experts actually verify agentic AI: familiar tools, not novel agent UI.

Fourteen contextual inquiries plus an observational study of ten spatial biologists using Claude Science on their own data produced four design directions: execution control, familiar interactive views, source/execution transparency, and accessible verification.

Experts verify by re-applying their own domain knowledge inside tools they already trust, not by learning an agent-native interface — a concrete provenance-surface decision for anyone building agentic tools for domain experts: show what the agent is doing right now, inside the view the expert already knows.

4. Interrupting a duplex voice model mid-response nearly doubles its jailbreak success rate.

Testing four open-source full-duplex models against 720 harmful requests, fixed-delay spoken interruption pushed whole-response attack success on AdvBench to 40.3% for PersonaPlex and 48.7% for PersonaPlex-RL — increases of 33.8 and 39.3 points over uninterrupted baselines. The failure only appears mid-turn, under a spoken cut-in, so a standard safety eval misses it entirely.

Anyone shipping interruptible voice needs interruption-specific guardrail testing before launch, not just the model’s off-the-shelf numbers. Kept past the usual 7-day window as a durable reliability-boundary finding.

5. The AI tutor design that looks most thoughtful — Socratic style plus full context — tested worst.

In a 132-student RCT, the Socratic AI teaching assistant with full context awareness was rated least favorably, with significantly lower perceived support than the other conditions, and the highest (though not statistically significant) observed rate of students bypassing it for an external LLM.

The intuitive pairing — maximal context awareness with maximal Socratic restraint — is the one that backfired. Worth testing guidance style and context depth as independent knobs rather than assuming they scale together.

This week

The forcing fork. Awareness-only interventions — flags, warnings, disclosure banners — move perception but not behavior, now corroborated across three studies at roughly 6,600 combined participants. What does move behavior is friction that forces an act before acceptance: a required written justification cut adoption of flawed AI suggestions 24 points with no cost to sound ones. Every disclosure regulation in reach (EU, Japan, California, Singapore) is satisfied by the failing, awareness-only class — the forcing pattern argues above the legal floor, not to it.

The metric that can’t tell help from retention. A field RCT (N=9,586), an automotive optimization loop, and two vendor postmortems all located engagement gains in the loop itself, not output quality: a warm system prompt raised chat rounds 24% but also raised content-free junk sessions 28%, and a trust-optimizing in-car loop’s gains evaporated once the optimizer stopped. Evaluate outcome quality separately from the interaction metric, with an evaluator tuned outside the loop that generated what’s judged.

Voice stops needing to break character to act. Vendor pricing (per-minute audio channels), model architecture (tool calls inside full-duplex speech without breaking turn-taking), and peer-reviewed ambient research (continuous step-aware AR guidance beating query-triggered help 21%+) converged on the same shift: voice and glasses interfaces no longer need to break conversational continuity to act. The open question is what the interface narrates while acting mid-conversation — narrating fake progress is a truthfulness violation, not a latency trick.
