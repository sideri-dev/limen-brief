1. Individual differences, not the type of CRUD action, predict whether someone delegates to an agent or does it themselves.

(single-source)

A peer-reviewed HAI 2026 study (N=73, 16 CRUD scenarios) found delegation tracks who the user is — individual differences explain about half the variance (ICC=.50) — not the type of CRUD operation.

That undercuts gating agent autonomy by task-risk tier: a per-user calibration step beats a risk-tiered approval gate. The venue check (HAI 2026’s own CFP, raw-fetched) confirms genuine peer review.

2. A persona that’s elicited through conversation, not authored, and expressed on a physical robot measurably raises trust.

(single-source)

A 25-person study with a humanoid robot (IEEE-RAS Humanoids 2026) found eliciting its persona through conversation, then expressing it physically rather than scripting a static profile, raised trust, anthropomorphism, consistency, relevance and overall quality — all five dimensions significant.

For embodied agents, how the persona is built and whether behavior expresses it is the lever, not chat-persona copywriting.

3. Anthropic replaced its two-program cyber-access setup with three identity-verified tiers, each with its own irreducible block list.

(single-source, vendor-primary)

The new Cyber Verification Program splits access into Defense, Red Team and Specialized tiers, scoped to the work authorized rather than one verified/unverified switch — even the loosest tier still blocks things like deploying ransomware.

The named tradeoff: enrollment requires mandatory data retention so Anthropic can monitor for misuse. A clean precedent for access control around dual-use capability — scoped tiers with a stated cost, not a binary gate.

4. A cheap model can own the expensive one’s KV cache, cutting time-to-first-token 5x.

(single-source)

RaReCache has a small model prefill a shared KV cache that a much larger model (up to 23x more parameters) reuses, recomputing only 30% of positions to keep 95–99% of its accuracy.

That cuts median time-to-first-token 5x, with up to 3x faster prefill and 1.8x more throughput per GPU — a measured recompute fraction to design a latency budget against.

5. Loading a 50-million-token session from local NVMe beats recomputing it — 2.8–4.3x faster and far cheaper on energy.

(single-source)

Tested with Gemma 4 12B and 31B on 50 million tokens of real text, loading cached state from encrypted local NVMe used 8.8–12.3x less GPU energy than recomputing it — reuse of stored state, not a wider attention window.

For products with long-running sessions (agent memory, long chat history), this is a measured alternative to re-running context or buying a bigger window.

This week

The confidence display has two unshipped preconditions, and “miscalibrated is worse than none” was never measured.

Before specifying a confidence UI, check two things: can the signal path even compute calibration (many vendor APIs expose no probabilities), and does the reporting party have any incentive to be honest (a model this week still claims high confidence 56% of the time when told it will likely fail). The widely-cited “miscalibrated is worse than none” claim was never tested. Where either check fails, spend the budget on verification-cost design instead.

Where the AI sits in a decision sequence, not disclosure or a mandated checkpoint, is the measured control for independent human judgment.

A system running on 260,739 real screening exams shows capturing the human’s judgment before showing the AI result, revealing it only on contradiction, is what preserves independence — mandated checkpoints without that ordering were 92.5% primed in a separate study. Japan has built the only regulated mechanism that hides the AI result until the human commits; the West has debated it without shipping it.

The ambient lane converged on one question: who’s inside the agent’s perceptual channel, and whether its boundary is legible.

Four studies locate the same gap in non-chat agents: entry into a listening channel is mostly solved, exit is nearly invisible to users, and richer social context for picking the right listener raises wrong-listener oversharing to 45% in one model. A peer-reviewed study adds the sharpest cost: people route corrections of the agent’s errors through channels it can’t sense, so its own telemetry under-counts failures.
