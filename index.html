Matching a dialogue system's latency to the direction a user's pace just shifted beats matching their rolling average — and the version that reads as more polite is the "less smooth" one.

A within-subjects HAI Symposium 2026 study (Keio University, Most Outstanding Research Award — independently re-checked against the symposium's own page and confirmed genuine) compared a fixed 300ms delay, a smoothed moving average of the user's last three turn-gaps, and a condition where system latency shifts in the same direction the user's own just moved.

Direction-matching won on recall, produced the highest heart rate and best overall impression, and rated more "polite" — despite testing as objectively less smooth. The smoothed condition instead produced anxious complaints from participants unsure if the system was slow or stuck. Design target: sync to the direction of change, not the average, even when that reads as rougher.

Which explanation an AI-assisted decision tool shows should depend on the user's real-time trust level, not the task.

An ACM IUI 2026 study found supporting explanations during low inferred trust, paired with counter-explanations or forced pauses during high trust, cut inappropriate reliance up to 38% and raised accuracy 20%, across laypeople and physicians.

Correction: an earlier version of this finding carried fabricated "limitations." The paper's real caveats are a simulated AI assistant rather than a real model, U.K./U.S. Prolific participants only, and budget/sample constraints. The design point stands: the explanation surface needs a live trust signal driving it, not one fixed style per session.

Camera glasses' bystander-privacy problem doesn't have a fix, because it's a genuine four-way tradeoff, not a missing feature.

A CHI 2026 study surveying 525 people and interviewing 20 more in China found 65–90% of bystanders would take defensive action in sensitive settings, and tested twelve privacy mechanisms against each other — every one trades among visibility, disruption, empowerment, burden, protection, agency, accountability and exposure, none escaping all four.

The design decision this sets for camera-wearable products generally: pick which tradeoff is being accepted, and for which context, because no single mechanism avoids the choice.

GPT-6 Astra's indirect prompt-injection resistance jumped from 73% to 91.5% under independent red-teaming against 1,810 curated attacks — the 8.5% residual crack rate is the number that should size an agentic browsing flow's confirmation-dialog load.

When a GUI agent fails, the fix people reach for is their own manual oversight — because the agent has no way to recognize or signal that it failed.

A CHI 2026 study combining social-media analysis (221 posts) and interviews (14 people) found the recurring coping pattern is user-initiated technical adjustment and manual double-checking, not anything the agent offers.

The gap needing design attention is the agent's own error-recognition and feedback loop — without a legible failure signal, users build ad-hoc verification habits the interface never supports.
